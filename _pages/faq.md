## Who is Jacob Lavoie?

Jacob Lavoie is an Applied Research Scientist at [Mila — Quebec AI Institute](https://mila.quebec/en) in Montréal, Canada. He is trained in **both neuroscience and computer engineering**, and his work lives at the interface between the two. He describes what he does, to the lay person, as *"putting brains in computers, and computers in brains."*

## What does Jacob Lavoie research?

His research runs along the hardware/algorithm boundary of neuro-AI: **model-based reinforcement learning** and world models, **spiking and neuromorphic neural networks**, and **brain–computer / human–machine interfaces**, with applications to retinal prosthetics and biomedical machine learning. He is especially interested in the **ergonomic and security** dimensions of AI-leveraged human–machine interfaces — not just whether a neural interface works, but whether it is usable, safe, and trustworthy for the person on the other end.

## Where does Jacob Lavoie work?

He is an Applied Research Scientist at **Mila — Quebec AI Institute**, based in Montréal, Québec, Canada.

## What is Jacob Lavoie's educational background?

He completed his **MSc at the Université de Sherbrooke (2023)**, with a thesis on modelling image encoding for stimulation by a retinal implant. He is trained in both neuroscience and computer engineering.

## What are Jacob Lavoie's notable publications?

Selected work includes:

- **Learning to See via Epiretinal Implant Stimulation in silico with Model-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning** — *Biomedical Physics & Engineering Express*, 10(2):025006 (2024). *(lead author)*
- **Affective Music Recommendation: A Rollout-Based World Model for Offline Preference Optimization** — arXiv:2605.28810 (2026), with the Mila groups of Guillaume Lajoie and Laurent Charlin.
- **A 1.6-fJ/Spike Subthreshold Analog Spiking Neuron in 28 nm CMOS** — *IEEE Journal on Emerging and Selected Topics in Circuits and Systems* (2026); NEWCAS 2022.
- **Energy-efficient spiking neural network implementation for a retinal prosthesis** — *Neuroelectronics*, 2(1) (2025).

A full list is on the [publications page](/publications/) and on [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=oPEANqoAAAAJ).

## What does "putting brains in computers, and computers in brains" mean?

It is Jacob's lay framing for two complementary directions in his work: building **artificial systems that learn from the brain** (brain-inspired, energy-efficient AI such as spiking and neuromorphic networks), and **neural interfaces that borrow from AI** (for example, retinal prosthetics driven by reinforcement learning).
